# Jurisdiction rules the desk applies (summary for case work; the panel confirms edge cases)

## Statutory annual leave and carry-over
| Country | Statutory minimum | Carry-over the law requires or allows |
|---|---|---|
| United Kingdom | 5.6 weeks (28 days incl. bank holidays) | The 4 EU-derived weeks carry over up to 18 months if sickness or family leave prevented taking them; the extra 1.6 weeks by agreement |
| Germany | 20 days (5-day week) | Carry to 31 March for operational or personal reasons; if sick, lapses 15 months after the leave year; employer must have prompted the employee to take leave or it does not lapse |
| France | 25 working days (30 ouvrables) | Carried when sickness or family leave prevented it; excess may go into a time-savings account (CET) where one exists |
| Netherlands | 4 × weekly hours | Statutory days lapse 6 months after year end unless the employee could not reasonably take them; extra-statutory days lapse after 5 years |
| Ireland | 4 working weeks | Carry within 6 months of year end by agreement; up to 15 months if prevented by sickness |
| Switzerland | 4 weeks (5 under 20) | Claims lapse after 5 years; no pay in lieu during employment |
| United States | None federally | Policy governs; California and Montana prohibit forfeiture of accrued vacation (caps on accrual are allowed) |
| Singapore | 7–14 days by service | Per contract; commonly 12 months to use |
| Hong Kong | 7–14 days by service | Must be granted within 12 months after the leave year |
| India | Varies by state Shops and Establishments Act (typically 12–18 days) | Accumulation caps (for example 45 days in Maharashtra); encashment rules apply |

## Working from another country
| Topic | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|
| Tax residence | 183 days in 12 months is the common threshold; some countries count differently (UK statutory residence test, Singapore 183 days in a calendar year) |
| Permanent establishment | A dependent agent who habitually concludes or plays the principal role in concluding contracts can create a PE in the host country, even on short stays |
| Social security | EU/EEA/Switzerland: A1 certificate keeps home-country cover up to 24 months. US has totalisation agreements with most EU countries, the UK, Switzerland, Japan, Korea, Canada, Australia; none with Singapore, Hong Kong, India (in force) or China |
| Right to work | Business visitors cannot do productive work beyond incidental activities; the Schengen 90/180-day rule limits stays for non-EU nationals |
| Data | Client-data localisation or secrecy applies in Switzerland (banking secrecy), Luxembourg, Singapore (banking secrecy, MAS outsourcing), Hong Kong; mainland China restricts data export |
